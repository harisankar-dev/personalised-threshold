# Phase 13 STP and budget regression

The Phase 13 edits are confined to English OCR and creative cue interpretation. The decision layer remains the research-informed, pre-launch Mode A advisory. Its option table still includes **A keep the current campaign, B revise one creative, C adjust eligible targeting, D test one variant, E consider multiple variants**. Keep is the comparator; the four aggregate consumer research profiles are context, not four recommended campaigns or targetable audience records.

The full 102-test regression includes Phase 11's 18 campaign states, cost arithmetic, fixed total budget, committed spend, headroom, revision/targeting/variant/segmentation/management costs, contribution margin, break-even extra conversions, tiny-reach warning, fractional-cent rejection and unknown-input handling. The real Chrome check separately verified an affordable revision (100 total, 80 committed, 10 revision cost) recommends one revision; the same cost with 95 committed holds. A committed-spend-greater-than-budget submission returned HTTP 400 and succeeded after correction. A user-entered two-extra-conversion scenario remained explicitly hypothetical and did not force rollout. No actual uplift is inferred.

Chrome checks also showed a behavioural campaign with an unknown source and one with unverified permission both held for review. The app continues to label consent as a marketer declaration and to warn about targeting-source mismatch or missing eligibility. Text, image, budget, scenario and mobile API responses remained compatible; no customer-file upload or live audience matching was added. Mode B is still an offline schema validator only.

The research dashboard and V1/V2/Criteo artifacts remain source-separated. No OSF respondent workbook was downloaded or used; Phase 10 rights and privacy gate remains in place. The [Phase 9 manifest](PHASE9_VERIFICATION_AND_PRESERVATION.md) verified all **604** protected source/model/result files unchanged after these edits. The five-level engine still returns `status: insufficient_evidence` and `recommended_level: None`; OCR and regex changes do not supply same-exposure consumer evidence for Levels 0–4.
