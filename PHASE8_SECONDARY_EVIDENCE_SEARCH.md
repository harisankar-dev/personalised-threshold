# Phase 8 — targeted secondary-evidence search

**49-C | 8 November 2026 | Scope:** linked ad condition, consumer response and engagement evidence. This is an inspection of published secondary sources, not new participant collection. It does not add respondent-level files to the project.

## Search and source verification

The search followed the two studies named in the Phase 8 brief, their publisher records, the institutional repository and the cited OSF replication project. The [location-targeting article](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00218499.2026.2709216) explicitly links [OSF project `zv8xy`](https://osf.io/zv8xy/); its [LMU record](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/138057/) has an abstract but no hosted full text or data. The [personalization-backfire paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12561546/) says respondent data are available **on request**, not through a verified public download. The search did not assume that an article table or supplementary appendix was participant-level data.

The OSF file tree was checked using the project's [public API record](https://api.osf.io/v2/nodes/zv8xy/) and [storage listing](https://api.osf.io/v2/nodes/zv8xy/files/osfstorage/). The replication README and three workbooks were read **in memory** to establish schema and aggregate quality; no raw OSF workbook, participant ID, IP address or row-level extract was saved in 49-C. The authors' short Study 1 and Study 2 R scripts were read to confirm the meanings of `PPR`, `PPI`, `PP` and `ITC`. The OSF project's `node_license` metadata is **null**, and its README contains no license. OSF's [licensing guidance](https://help.osf.io/article/148-licensing) warns that unlicensed shared work has unclear reuse rights. Public accessibility was therefore not treated as a commercial or redistribution grant. Author or repository clarification is required before retaining the records, fitting a reusable model or distributing derived artifacts. The workbook's direct identifiers make a privacy review necessary as well.

## Candidate A — Verenkotte and Spann, location specificity and transparency

**Article:** [*How Information-Use Transparency Affects the Effectiveness of Location Targeting*](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00218499.2026.2709216), DOI `10.1080/00218499.2026.2709216`, published online 25 September 2026. **Repository:** [OSF `zv8xy`](https://osf.io/zv8xy/). **License:** no explicit project data license in API or README; article access does not establish workbook reuse terms.

The repository also lists `README.txt`, `study1_replication.R`, `study2_replication.R`, `study3_replication.R`, `suppl_analysis_12.R` and copies of `process.R`. The online experiments manipulate **two** factors in 2×2 designs. Study 1 compares no location targeting with high specificity (city) targeting; Study 2 compares low specificity (country) with high specificity (city). Both cross these with no information-use disclosure versus disclosure. Study 3 tests low/high specificity and disclosure using ad-set aggregate click counts. These are **three study-specific location conditions across two experiments**, not the project's five generic/contextual/behavioural/historical/combined levels. They are not the uploaded marketer creative or its actual audience. The publication states that Study 2 used desktop respondents and a running-event ad; product interest is not broadly measured. No consent/eligibility flag for a marketer's intended audience is present. The lab outcomes are ratings and **click intention**; actual clicks are only aggregate in Study 3. There is no consumer-level exposure denominator tied to direct intrusion responses in the field file.

**Non-identifying inspection only:** condition-cell sizes for Study 1 (`Targ`, `Trans` coded 0/1) were **107, 98, 98, 106** for `(0,0), (0,1), (1,0), (1,1)`; Study 2 sizes were **100, 100, 100, 101**. All 810 inspected respondent rows have observed `Targ`, `Trans`, `ITC`, `PPR`, `PPI` and `PP`. Study 1 cell means for `PPR` were **3.065, 3.446, 3.272, 3.308** and for `PPI` **2.561, 3.542, 3.241, 4.028** in that order. Study 2 cell means for `PPR` were **2.511, 2.642, 3.100, 2.910** and for `PPI` **2.413, 3.088, 3.107, 3.595**. These are observed descriptive questionnaire means, **not** a new causal effect estimate, held-out prediction or universal ranking of targeting methods. The published paper's design and analysis should govern substantive interpretation.

**Recommendation:** strong **research candidate**, but **do not integrate or retain respondent records yet**. Confirm a usable data license, rights to research derivatives and an identifier-minimized handling plan. With those conditions met, the narrowest defensible supervised task would be predicting or comparing **direct privacy intrusiveness for the specific location-targeting/disclosure stimuli**, using only pre-exposure condition and justified baseline features. Split by respondent and study, preserve an untouched holdout, report high-intrusion false reassurance and compare against a training-mean baseline. Do not use `PPR`, `PP` or `ITC` as pre-launch predictors of `PPI`: they are post-exposure outcomes. Do not relabel the study's conditions as five 49-C levels or deploy estimates to unrelated ads.

## Candidate B — Kim and Ha, personalization backfire experiment

The [publisher/PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12561546/) is DOI `10.3390/bs15101323`. It reports **360 South Korean respondents** in a **3 personalization-message × 2 situational-privacy-prime** randomized design. Message conditions are generic, contextual location and a high condition combining name/location/purchase details. Its measured dependent outcome is **purchase intention**, with perceived personalization, state privacy concern and trust/experience questions. The paper does **not** provide a downloadable respondent-level dataset or a measured direct ad-intrusiveness target; its data-availability statement says **upon request from the corresponding author**. The article's appendix/stimulus text is not a substitute for row-level outcomes. License for respondent-level data: **not established**. The medium and high stimuli change both information amount and type, which the authors acknowledge as a design confound. **Recommendation: use as bounded published context, exclude from training and threshold validation.** No author contact or primary collection was undertaken.

## Search decision

The OSF experiment closes **part** of the central gap at the laboratory-response level: condition, direct relevance, direct privacy intrusiveness and click intention are in the same record. It does **not** close the license/privacy, real campaign audience, actual-click, multiple-ad, direct-consent or five-level gaps. Because a supervised model cannot be retained and reused responsibly under the present terms, Phase 8 did **not** train one or download raw records into the project. The next concrete action is to obtain explicit OSF data-reuse terms and a minimized, licensed version of the experiment or find an equivalent openly licensed linked dataset. Only then should a new, isolated experimental model be considered.
