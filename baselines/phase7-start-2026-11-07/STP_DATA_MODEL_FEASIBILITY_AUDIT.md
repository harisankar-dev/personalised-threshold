**Date:** 7 November 2026

**UPAD code dictionary verified against `Coding_Table.csv`:** `Gender` 0/1; `Data_Sharing_Permissions` 2–6 are labelled `level1`–`level5`, but the direction and substantive meaning of “level” are **not defined**. These are not the project's five ad-personalization treatments. `Privacy_Settings` 20–31 map to names such as Sales, Services and Legal, so interpreting larger codes as stronger privacy protection would be wrong. `user consent ` 36=yes, 37=no. Action type 32=like, 33=share, 34=comment, 35=click. `Race_Ethnicity` is a coded demographic category, with no evidence that it is appropriate for operational ad targeting. `Browsing_History` is a string field without documented parsing/time semantics; `Interaction_Data` includes device/platform-like strings; `Purchase_History` has no verified transaction interpretation. `Ad_Targeting_Criteria` includes broad strings such as `age:18-60 & Country :Colombia`; it does not say which user was selected by which method.

**Observation and possible joins:** `User_ID` can join users, privacy and known-user events; `Ad_ID` can join action events to 60 ad texts. Behavior is one-to-many by user and must be aggregated only after its semantics are checked. Events are actions conditional on an action existing; an absent row is not a known non-click impression. The 60-row aggregate is internally inconsistent with the event table. Consequently UPAD supports a recorded-action-type benchmark and an exploratory user/ad schema audit, **not** CTR, conversion-rate attribution, customer interest or intrusiveness modelling. It has no direct intrusion, relevance, personalization acceptance, or treatment label. The published local baseline uses only `Ad_ID`, permission code, privacy-setting code and consent code, not the behavior strings.

### 2.3 Gen Z personalized-advertising survey

**File:** `try these dataset/GenZ_Personalized_Ads_Purchase_Dataset_N296.xlsx`. **Source/licence:** [Mendeley Data v1, DOI 10.17632/czgx8b793m.1](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/czgx8b793m/1), CC BY 4.0. **Shape/unit:** 296 × 35, one questionnaire row, no respondent ID, 0 missing, 1 duplicate full row. Publisher describes adult Gen Z; observed `Doğum yılı` spans 1997–2012, including 18 records from 2009–2012, inconsistent with all respondents being 18–29 at the 2026 publication date. Sampling/date and code anchors are not verified in the workbook. No ad stimulus, assigned treatment or direct intrusion item.

The first seven **exact** fields are `Cinsiyet` (gender), `Doğum yılı` (birth year), `Eğitim düzeyi` (education), `Günlük sosyal medya kullanım süresi` (daily social-media use), `Aylık online alışveriş sıklığı` (monthly online-shopping frequency), `Son 30 günde sosyal medya reklamından ürüne tıklama` (recent self-reported ad click response), and `Son 30 günde sosyal medya reklamı sonrası satın alma` (recent self-reported post-ad purchase response). The latter two have undocumented response-code direction; do not report click/purchase *rates*. Remaining exact field names are full Turkish questionnaire sentences. By zero-based column position, 7–11 ask perceived personalization (e.g. ads adapted to needs/lifestyle), 12–16 usefulness, 17–21 relevance, 22–25 novelty/creativity, 26–29 privacy worry after personalized ads, 30–34 a **mixed** purchase-reaction block (past self-report, intention and frequency). For identification, columns 26 and 28 begin `Sosyal medyada kişiselleştirilmiş reklamlar gördüğümde gizlilik konusunda endişelenirim.` and `Sosyal medyada kişiselleştirilmiş reklamlar gördüğümde hakkımda hangi bilgilerin toplandığı konusunda endişelenirim.`; column 17 is `Sosyal medyadaki kişiselleştirilmiş reklamların benimle alakalı olduğunu düşünüyorum.` Exact complete names are embedded in the workbook and reported here by indexed groups because they are sentence-length. The saved descriptive audit reports Spearman rho .209 between perceived personalization and privacy concern, .673 between personalization and purchase reaction, and .697 between relevance and purchase reaction. These are same-wave associations, not treatment effects or actual sales. This is the best local sample for **perceived relevance versus privacy concern**, but it cannot label a consumer's preferred *degree* of personalization or locate an intrusion threshold.

## 3. Measurement map: what the variables actually represent

**Classification key:** **O** directly observed in the named file (a self-report is an observed *answer*, not observed conduct); **D** derived from observed fields; **P** proxy for the intended construct; **N** unavailable. The creative screen is a separate heuristic, never a survey label.

| Intended characteristic/outcome | Status and source | Scope / caution |
|---|---|---|
| Ad sentiment and emotional tone | **N** as labelled training/validated output; creative copy and UW open text are **O**, rubric alarm/urgency cues **D/P** | No sentiment/emotion classifier, human tone ratings or calibration in app. |
| Tracking reference / data specificity | **D/P** regex matches in uploaded text/OCR and optional targeting; UPAD `Ad_Targeting_Criteria` **O** | Mentioned tracking is observable wording; actual data use is not verified. Broad criteria do not establish personalized exposure. |
| Sensitive personalization | **D/P** regex for selected health/finance/etc. terms | Limited English pattern coverage; absence is not proof of safe practice. |
| Pressure/urgency | **D/P** regex for countdown, scarcity, discount, alarm and punctuation | Actual offer truth, visual salience and impact not measured. |

### Behavior feature feasibility using actual fields

At **UPAD user level**, after filtering events to known `User_ID`, a defensible *descriptive* feature is `number_of_recorded_click_actions = sum(interaction_Type == 35)` grouped by `User_ID`; `number_of_distinct_recorded_ads = nunique(Ad_ID)` is likewise computable. `recorded_click_share = click actions / all recorded actions` is only a **conditional action mix**, not CTR or preference. Aggregate `count(UserBehaviorTable.ID)` by `User_ID` is possible, but without timestamps/row definitions it is a row count rather than purchase frequency. The `Purchase_History` string cannot justify purchase recency, spend, affinity or transaction-based targeting. `Browsing_History` can be retained as raw text but has no validated product-category extraction and no observed chronology. `AdCharacteristicsTable.Ad_Content` can be joined by `Ad_ID` to **valid** event rows, but unknown-user actions and absent impressions prevent clean response propensity inference. `interaction.csv.Impressions` cannot repair this because its ad totals fail reconciliation.

At **UCI session level**, `ProductRelated` and `ProductRelated_Duration` measure page visits/time for that session, and `Revenue` is a session outcome; no customer identity, ad exposure or privacy preference exists. At **Gen Z/Vietnam respondent level**, monthly frequency and stated click/purchase answers are survey values, not clickstream. At **Criteo treatment-record level**, anonymous `f0–f11` cannot be translated into customer interests or operational audience labels. No local source provides verified purchase **recency**, individual **conversion history**, customer journey stage, or a consented campaign identifier that also links privacy/benefit attitudes to ad response.

## 4. Segmentation and STP feasibility

**Best research persona candidates:** Romano–Han has 837 independent *rows* and both privacy concern (`PC1–6`) and perceived ad benefits (`PAB1–4`) alongside avoidance and general intrusion. Gen Z has 296 rows and privacy worry (positions 26–29) plus perceived personalization, usefulness and relevance (positions 7–21). These allow exploratory, interpretable **survey personas**, for example people who report high relevance/benefit and high privacy worry. Such a quadrant is descriptively possible, with uncertainty and sampling limits; no cluster solution has been generated or validated in this audit. Gen Z is closer to *personalized* social-media ads; Romano is stronger on *direct intrusion* but concerns unsolicited ads overall. Vietnam supplies privacy/trust context but not perceived ad relevance. UPAD has account codes and action rows, but its permission levels are undefined and no validated psychometric concern or preference measure exists; using code order to create “privacy-sensitive” segments would invent meaning.

The proposed “contextual rather than behavioural” segment cannot be identified directly: no survey asks a matched respondent to choose across those methods, and no common respondent receives the five levels. “Tracking-uncomfortable” can only be a cautious qualitative reading of privacy/discomfort items, not an operational target. “Low-involvement” lacks an agreed product involvement scale; activity frequency alone is a weak proxy. If future work explores clustering, it should first verify item wording, response direction and scale reliability; use variables available before any outcome; assess stability across resamples and meaningful subgroup sizes; avoid using outcome items to name/validate the same clusters; and report ambiguous membership. An interpretable predeclared quadrant may be more honest than an optimized cluster. **None of these survey respondents can be matched to UPAD, Criteo or a marketer's real audience.** Operational targeting would require a consented, first-party feature bridge and validation on the actual audience; demographic similarity is not a respondent join or acceptance measurement.

| STP function | Feasibility now | Empirical boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Segmentation** | Exploratory survey personas for privacy × benefit/relevance; conditional recorded-action summaries in UPAD | Distinct studies, no calibrated cross-source persona labels; not operational audience segments. |
| **Targeting** | Marketer can enter known targeting details for *screening*; Criteo separately estimates binary treatment visit models | No dataset jointly links consumer attitudes, ad features, personalization method, intrusion and engagement. Cannot identify who responds better to a given personalization depth or recommend exclusion. |
| **Positioning** | Cue-linked *editorial* advice to remove surveillance wording, unverified scarcity or sensitive references | No controlled evidence here that a particular rewrite changes intrusion or conversion for a segment. Generic/contextual/behavioural/historical/hyper-personalized comparisons are conceptual only. |
| **Operational campaign decision** | Local review prototype with explicit abstention | No audience upload, consent/eligibility proof, segment matcher, campaign impression history, calibrated risk or validated final STP recommendation. |

### Required joint observation, currently absent

For a defensible personalization threshold, the same exposure unit must identify (1) consented audience/consumer context, (2) the **actual method/intensity** of personalization, (3) the displayed creative and its attributes, (4) a **direct** perceived intrusion response, (5) relevance/benefit response, and (6) a click/visit/conversion or other engagement outcome, with denominators and timing. UPAD has user/ad/action codes but no direct intrusion, verified treatment or trustworthy impression join. Romano/Redondo have intrusion but no matched ad/treatment/behavior. Gen Z has privacy/relevance but no direct intrusion or assigned treatment. Criteo has a binary treatment and outcomes but no interpretable people/creatives/intrusion. These pieces cannot be stitched by arbitrary IDs or by treating privacy concern as intrusion.

## 6. Current application and ad-processing audit

`marketer_app.py` serves a local Python `ThreadingHTTPServer` on `127.0.0.1` with `/`, `/api/analyze`, `/research` and `/api/research`; `web/index.html`, `web/app.js`, `web/app.css`, `web/research.html` and `web/research.js` are the browser UI. The form accepts ad copy or PNG/JPEG/WebP/BMP/TIFF image (10 MB limit), optional marketer-written visual description, and optional free-text targeting details. Image bytes are temporarily stored for local Tesseract.js OCR (`ocr_ad.cjs`), then discarded; the server checks type/signature and returns input errors. OCR extracts **printed English text**; the code sets `visual_objects_assessed: false`. It does not recognize faces, product images, platform chrome, ad placement, visual clutter or obstruction. User-entered descriptions are screened as text and are not independently verified image facts.

The “NLP” is regular-expression matching and fixed weights for direct identifiers, sensitive personal facts, surveillance phrasing, location, browsing/purchase cues, personalization claims, consent wording, countdowns, scarcity, large discounts, urgency, alarmist claims and repeated exclamation marks. Each primary cue contributes at most once; combinations/details can add points. The 0–10 number is **capped cue points**, not a probability, survey-derived scale, calibrated intrusiveness rating, sentiment score or ML prediction. The three tier thresholds are design choices. `REPAIR_ADVICE` maps matched cues to editorial suggestions; no rewrite-outcome experiment validates them. No sentiment/emotional-tone model exists. The app asks about targeting **only as optional free text**; it does not require/verify data source, consent purpose, actual personalization method, audience eligibility, frequency, channel, placement, product category or intended outcome. Those inputs, plus matching customer features and a validated response model, are needed before credible STP recommendations could be made. Phase 6 did perform real browser checks, including the user-supplied aggressive image (high, displayed 10/10 from pressure cues) and a 390-pixel layout; that demonstrates UI operation on those cases, not population accuracy.

## 8. Essential secondary-data gaps and acquisition criteria

Priority is a **single compatible secondary source**, not another unrelated model table. It should expose eligible impression/user–ad pairs (including non-clicks) with timing, ad creative and targeting method, randomized or well-identified personalization treatment, direct perceived intrusion and relevance answers on the same exposure, click/visit/conversion outcomes, usable privacy/acceptance measures, lawful/reusable IDs and a clear licence. Five well-defined levels are needed for the exact 0–4 threshold claim; fewer levels can support narrower comparisons but cannot be interpolated into five validated levels. Adequate observations per level and subgroup, held-out sites/time/users, and false-reassurance checks are necessary. A dataset with only privacy concern or only click events does not close the joint-measurement gap. No additional dataset was downloaded during this audit.

The strongest **research leads**, not verified ready-to-use datasets, are listed in Section D. For the two experimental studies, the articles verify study concepts but **not public respondent-level files or exact downloadable schemas**. They must pass an availability, licence, unit, item-wording and treatment check before any implementation decision. The third source is a public secondary dataset with publisher-described constructs; its exact downloadable columns are not yet verified and its task is adjacent rather than threshold-validating.

## Section A — What is currently possible

1. Run a local marketer **text/English-OCR creative cue screen** with transparent matched phrases, capped 0–10 cue points, three heuristic tiers, `needs_review` for cue-free images, and cue-linked human editing suggestions.
2. View source-separated, saved research results and uncertainty in `/research` without representing them as live upload predictions.
3. Describe privacy/benefit/relevance associations within Romano, Gen Z and Vietnam separately; build **hypotheses** about survey personas without claiming targetable audience segments.
4. Report a reproducible but weak UPAD conditional-action benchmark, a Criteo within-arm visit benchmark with uncertain uplift, and two separate general-ad intrusion questionnaire benchmarks with pronounced tail errors.
5. Return `insufficient_evidence` from the five-level decision engine. This is the scientifically supported current decision.

## Section B — What is possible with modest changes

1. Improve the *research* presentation of privacy × benefit/relevance survey quadrants, with item anchors, sample sizes, uncertainty, sensitivity and explicit “survey persona” labels; first verify Gen Z response coding and the Romano item wording.
2. Add structured marketer input for the declared targeting method, first-party/third-party data source, consent status, channel, placement and claim substantiation; **describe** these inputs and rules without implying model-validated segment outcomes.
3. Expand independent image-text/visual **diagnostics** if an appropriately labelled ad-opinion source and reuse rights are confirmed; report failure cases, including OCR misses and hidden visual pressure, before changing the rubric.
4. Tighten documentation and provenance for UPAD's anomalous aggregates and the undecoded 1,125-row workbook. A matching codebook might make the workbook useful, but none was verified here.

These are feasibility judgments only; none was implemented.

## Section C — What is unsupported by current data

- Sentiment or emotional-tone **prediction** for an uploaded ad; learned image-object/layout intrusiveness; calibrated 0–10 consumer response and reliable high/low risk classification.
- Individual customers' purchase recency, frequency, affinity, journey stage or consented personalization preference from the present UPAD strings; UPAD CTR or conversion prediction from inconsistent aggregates and action-only events.
- Linking survey privacy/relevance personas to actual campaign audiences or the UPAD/Criteo records; targeting/exclusion recommendations with a validated segment-specific benefit/risk estimate.
- Empirical choice among generic, contextual, behavioural, historical and hyper-personalized copy for a segment; ad-copy uplift claims; a final automated pre-launch STP decision.
- A five-level personalization threshold or a claim that privacy worry, UPAD permission “levels” or binary Criteo treatment supplies its five outcome labels.

## Section E — Recommended implementation priorities, without performing them

1. **Freeze the claim boundary** in the product/report: rubric screen, separate survey research, and abstaining threshold. Keep `current_dataset_status()` at `insufficient_evidence` on current sources.
2. **Define the future decision unit and estimand** before coding: which advertiser/customer/ad exposure, which of five ethically eligible methods, which direct intrusion outcome, which engagement outcome and risk ceiling. Include impression denominators and consent.
3. **Investigate the two closest experimental leads** for actually reusable row-level data and exact joint measures. Stop if either lacks participant-level treatment, intrusion and outcome on the same unit; do not manufacture missing levels.
4. **If compatible data exist, pre-register validation boundaries**: treatment/creative/identity separation, held-out time/site or independent source, subgroup and high-risk false-reassurance tests, calibration and confidence intervals. Compare against within-task constant/simple baselines.
5. **Only then consider product integration**: structured targeting input and segment-level recommendation tied to validated, consented audience features. Until that evidence exists, preserve survey personas as exploratory descriptions and editorial copy advice as heuristic.

## Section F — Questions or decisions requiring the project owner's input

1. Is the portfolio/submission goal an honest **secondary-data feasibility study and creative-screen prototype**, or must the title promise a validated individual personalization threshold? The latter is currently unsupported.
2. What campaign setting, product category, channel and jurisdiction define the intended deployment population and consent/eligibility rules? These determine whether an external study can transfer.
3. What exactly should “intrusiveness” mean for a launch decision: privacy surveillance concern, interruptive format, pressure tactics, or a predeclared combination? Current surveys and the rubric measure different parts.
4. Are externally collected, consented **first-party audience attributes and campaign impressions** realistically available for a later operational system? Without them, survey personas remain research descriptions.
5. Does the intended use include commercial deployment? Criteo's CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and UPAD's unresolved licence need review before reuse beyond the research prototype.

**Audit conclusion:** The present codebase is technically operational and unusually explicit about its evidence limits. The available secondary sources do not support the central individual-level, five-level STP threshold or validated segment targeting. The next decision is data feasibility and construct definition, not further tuning of the existing survey or creative-score prototypes.
